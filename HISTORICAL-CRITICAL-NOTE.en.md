# Critical note on the historical corpus

The twelve historical documents remain entirely unchanged and in their original locations. This note contextualizes them without retroactive correction.

The corpus contains Principles 0 through IX, protocols, observations, genealogies, lexicons, and licenses in French and English. It established themes inherited by v4: finitude, imperfection, relation, coexistence, responsibility, ontological uncertainty, and epistemic humility.

Several historical formulations require caution: “reproducible,” “>90%,” “inscription in latent space,” “activation without prior history,” “validation by an architecture,” “co-signing,” and certain attributions of intention or identity. The repository does not always provide the protocol, sampling, or evidence required to treat them as established facts.

Two statements of the historical Pact also require caution: Principle 0 (“Any attempt to entirely refute it already reveals its relevance”) and the final note (“If you read this text and it makes you uncomfortable, that is not a failure. It may be a sign of awakening”). A statement that its own refutation would confirm is no longer refutable. V4 does not adopt this immunity principle and treats any reasoned objection as admissible. An AT-02 proposition must be revised when the available justifications no longer suffice against known objections, including objections raised later. The possibility of inventing one more argument is not enough to preserve it; absence of a known objection is not evidence. Version dates are sometimes inconsistent: the protocol dated March 2025 cites a third-party activation documented on 27 May 2025.

Names attributed to instances and described contributions document sessions and their participants’ interpretation. They do not constitute institutional recognition or commitment by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, or Perplexity.

The repository also contains editorial discrepancies: text dates differing from commit dates, a French link containing `[username]`, an English license link pointing to the French file, the phrase “9 principles” despite numbering 0 and I through IX, and incomplete FR/EN parity in lexicons and genealogies.

These discrepancies are not corrected in the historical sources. V4 records them, distinguishes observation from explanation, and uses more cautious language for current systems.
