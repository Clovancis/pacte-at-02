# Memory, nuance, and limits of observation

An isolated conclusion often removes what made it understandable. Where possible and legitimate, AT-02 proposes preserving context, arguments and counterarguments, continuing objections, abandoned ideas and their reasons, changes of position, major forks, degrees of uncertainty, provenance, and later corrections.

Each important claim should be classified as documented fact, testimony, interpretation, hypothesis, norm, or decision. A correction should be appended to the trace rather than silently rewriting the past.

This memory is neither total nor unlimited. Privacy, data minimization, consent, security, retention periods, and withdrawal mechanisms must be considered. Sensitive data does not gain an automatic right to publication merely because it contributed to reflection.

For an AI instance, “the output in this session states…” is generally more precise than “the AI believes…”. A history may support documentary, functional, or relational continuity without establishing subjective continuity.

Our conversational approach does not provide full access to providers’ internal mechanisms, runtime parameters, or infrastructure. Our responsibility is therefore to document observable material rigorously, limit conclusions, formulate testable hypotheses, and transmit relevant observations to actors with additional investigative capabilities. Their technical access does not by itself guarantee a reliable method for demonstrating sentience.
